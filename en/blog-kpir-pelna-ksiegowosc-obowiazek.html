KPiR or full accounting - when does the obligation to transfer arise

Check when you need to switch to full accounting, how to calculate the limit and how to prepare documents and processes in the company.

Accounting January 10, 2023 Liber Pro Accounting

The transition from KPiR to full accounting is not only an organizational decision - in certain situations it results from regulations. From the perspective of an accounting office, what is important is not only the limit itself, but also the advance preparation of an implementation plan, document circulation and the method of keeping records from the new year.

When does the obligation to switch to full accounting arise?

The limit of net sales revenues is crucial. Pursuant to the Accounting Act, the obligation to keep full accounting books arises when net sales revenues exceed 2,000,000 euros in the previous financial year.

Limit: 2,000,000 euros per year

After it was exceeded this year - in next year you are obliged to keep accounting books in accordance with the Accounting Act.

How to calculate the limit correctly?

  • The limit is counted from net revenues from sales (excluding VAT)
  • Converted by NBP exchange rate from the last business day of the previous tax year
  • Applies to previous tax year — not the current one
  • It matters moment of exceedance, not an annual average

For example: if the EUR/PLN exchange rate on the last business day of 2025 was PLN 4.25, the revenue limit in PLN is 2,000,000 × 4.25 = PLN 8,500,000. Exceeding this amount in 2025 means the obligation to complete accounting from January 1, 2026.

What changes in practice for the company?

1. More record-keeping obligations

  • Recording of each economic event according to accounting principles
  • Obligation to prepare and implement accounting policy
  • Closing monthly and annual reporting periods
  • Annual financial statements submitted to the National Court Register and the tax office

2. Greater management responsibility

  • The Management Board is responsible for the reliability and correctness of the financial statements
  • Obligation to sign the report with an electronic signature
  • For larger entities - obligation to audit by a certified auditor

3. Better financial control

  • Accurate and up-to-date picture of the company's assets and financial situation
  • Better data for business decisions, investments and planning
  • Higher credibility towards banks, investors and contractors

Can I switch to full accounting earlier?

Yes. And in many cases this is beneficial, even if the company has not yet reached the €2 million limit. It is worth considering voluntary transfer when:

  • Company is growing dynamically and the limit is a matter of the next 1-2 years
  • You're planning investments or investor entry — needs full financial data
  • You're trying to... bank loan for a higher amount
  • You want to have it a complete picture of costs and profitability individual projects or departments

Full accounting is not just an obligation - it is a management tool. Companies that switch to it consciously and in advance often discover that they have better insight into their finances than they previously thought.

Does every company have to keep full accounting?

Yes - regardless of your income level. Full accounting is mandatory for all:

  • Limited liability companies (sp. z o. o.)
  • Joint stock companies (S.A.)
  • Simple joint stock companies (P.S.A.)
  • Limited partnerships and limited joint-stock partnerships

A general partnership and a professional partnership may maintain KPiR until the limit of EUR 2 million is exceeded - provided that none of the partners is a legal person.

The most common mistakes when switching to full accounting

Error 1. Ignoring limit exceedance

The company exceeds the threshold of EUR 2 million, but still maintains KPiR. Effect: invalid books, risk of tax penalties, need to keep two parallel records backwards.

Error 2. Late system preparation

The transition to full accounting requires the selection of software, implementation of accounting policy and training of responsible persons. Starting this in December for January is a recipe for chaos.

Mistake 3. Treating full accounting only as an expense

Full accounting costs more than KPiR - that's a fact. But it also provides much more management information. Companies that treat it as an investment rather than an expense tend to make better business decisions faster.

What to prepare before moving on?

It is best to plan the transition to full accounting at least 2-3 months in advance. To-do list:

  • Choosing accounting software supporting full accounting books (e.g. Comarch ERP Optima)
  • Development of accounting policy — principles of asset valuation, financial period, depreciation methods
  • Inventory of assets — fixed assets, inventories, receivables, liabilities as at the opening day
  • Opening balance — statement of assets and liabilities as of the first day of the new year
  • Chart of accounts adapted to the specific nature of the company
  • Training people responsible for documents and invoices

It is best to do this before the end of the year so that you can start keeping full books from January 1 without any arrears.

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Does the EUR 2 million limit apply to gross or net revenues?

Net - excluding VAT. The limit is calculated on net sales revenues, converted at the National Bank of Poland exchange rate from the last business day of the previous year.

Is it possible to return to KPiR after revenues fall below the limit?

Yes, if the revenues have not exceeded the limit and the company is not obliged to complete accounting due to its legal form. Limited liability company always maintains full accounting - regardless of revenues.

Does full accounting mean different recordkeeping rules?

No. It changes the way of recording, not the tax rate. It provides a more complete picture of the financial situation and requires more extensive accounting documentation.

Do banks require full accounting for a loan?

Often yes, especially for higher amounts. Full accounting gives the bank a more reliable picture of the company's financial situation than the KPiR.

Summary

The moment of switching to full accounting is one of the most important stages of company development. It's not just an obligation - it's a change in the way we manage our finances, with greater transparency and greater accountability. Companies that prepare for this transition in advance and treat it as an opportunity, not a problem - gain real benefits: better data, higher credibility and more efficient management.
