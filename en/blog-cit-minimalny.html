Minimum CIT – who it may apply to and how to check the conditions

From 2024, minimum CIT may apply to some companies with a loss or low profitability. Check what conditions and data need to be verified.

Taxes January 11, 2024 Liber Pro Accounting

Minimum CIT does not mean that every company with a loss will pay additional tax. In practice, it is crucial to check whether statutory conditions are met, whether exemptions apply and what the company's operating profitability looks like. From the perspective of an accounting office, what is most important is a reliable analysis of financial data before the end of the year.

Where did the minimum CIT come from?

Minimum CIT was introduced into the CIT Act together with the Polish Order on January 1, 2022. However, it aroused so much controversy that the legislator immediately suspended its use for two years. From January 1, 2024, the regulations returned in a changed and slightly relaxed form.

Who may be subject to minimum CIT?

The minimum CIT covers companies that are CIT taxpayers and which in a given tax year:

Condition 1 – Operating loss

They suffered a loss from a source of income other than capital gains (operating loss).

Condition 2 – Low profitability

They achieved a share of income in revenues (operating profitability) of no more than 2%.

The tax applies mainly to: limited liability companies, joint-stock companies, simple joint-stock companies, limited partnerships and limited joint-stock partnerships, and tax capital groups.

Who is excluded from minimum CIT?

  • Companies in the year of establishment and for the next 2 tax years
  • Small taxpayers – revenues below EUR 2 million
  • Companies with 100% shareholding by natural persons (without shares in other companies)
  • Companies subject to restructuring or bankruptcy proceedings
  • Entities using Estonian CIT
  • Companies whose revenues decreased by over 30% year on year
  • Companies that achieved profitability above 2% in at least one of the last 3 years

How are ground conditions established?

This is not a classic income tax - that is why it is surprising for many companies. The tax base is calculated in two ways (the taxpayer chooses the more favorable one):

Method 1 (basic)Method 2 (simplified)
Basis1.5% of revenues + costs of debt financing from related entities + costs of intangible services from related entities3% of the value of operating revenues
Tax rate10% of the tax base
Payment deadlineUntil the end of March of the following year (together with CIT-8)

Example – loss and additional burden

Scenario (simplified, method 2):

Operating revenues PLN 5,000,000
Operating loss -100,000 PLN
Profitability -2% → below the threshold
CIT basis min. (method 2): 3% × 5,000,000 PLN 150,000
Minimum CIT to be paid (10%) PLN 15,000

The company shows a loss of PLN 100,000 and still pays PLN 15,000 in tax. The tax paid can be deducted from the classic CIT within the next 3 years - as long as the company returns to profitability.

Why is it worth checking the data in advance?

Many companies assume that if the loss results from economically justified investments or a difficult year, there will be no tax. Meanwhile, minimal CIT does not ask about the cause of the loss. It only checks the numerical result. If the profitability is below 2% and there is no statutory exemption, tax arises.

Companies that are particularly vulnerable are:

  • They invest intensively and generate high depreciation costs
  • They use debt financing from related entities
  • They have a significant share of intangible services from related entities (management, licenses)
  • They operate in difficult industries with low margins
  • Have gone through a difficult year due to external reasons (inflation, loss of contract)

The most common risks and errors when assessing minimum CIT

Mistake 1. Lack of awareness of duty

The company does not analyze profitability during the year. One learns about the minimum CIT only when preparing CIT-8 - too late to take any action.

Mistake 2. Failure to monitor the profitability ratio

Profitability is not monitored quarterly. The company does not know that it exceeded the risk threshold in Q3 and could have taken action before the year ended.

Mistake 3. Focusing only on the classic CIT tax

The management board only looks at "whether we have a profit and how much CIT we will pay" - without analyzing whether the company is subject to minimum CIT at the same time.

Mistake 4: Acting after the fact

The analysis for minimum CIT is performed only at the end of the year - with no possibility of influencing the result. Tax planning must be conducted throughout the year.

How to check whether the minimum CIT applies to your company?

Step by step before closing the year:

1

Calculate the profitability ratio

Operating Income ÷ Operating Revenue. If the result is below 2% or negative, you enter the minimum CIT risk zone.

2

Check statutory exclusions

Are you a small taxpayer (revenue below EUR 2 million)? Has the company existed for less than 3 years? Have you had a yield above 2% in any of the last 3 years? Each exclusion exempts you from minimum CIT.

3

Calculate your potential tax using both methods

Compare the result of the basic method (1.5% of revenues + adjustments) with the simplified method (3% of revenues). Choose a lower one.

4

Assess scheduling options (if time)

If the year is still running - is it possible to increase the profitability to 2%? Can the cost structure be optimized? It's best to do this with your advisor before the year closes.

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Does the minimum CIT apply to every company?

NO. Applies only to CIT companies (limited liability companies, joint-stock companies and similar). It does not apply to JDGs, civil partnerships, general partnerships or entities settling PIT. Small entities with revenues below EUR 2 million will not pay it either.

How to check whether a company benefits from minimum CIT exemptions?

Often yes - when planning during the year, not after the end of the year. Analyzing statutory exemptions and optimizing your cost structure can eliminate the obligation.

Does the minimum CIT apply to companies with a loss?

Yes – this is the main case. A company with an operating loss may be obliged to pay minimum CIT calculated on revenues, not on income.

Does minimum CIT affect financial liquidity?

Yes, this is a real cash cost. The tax paid can be deducted from the classic CIT within the next 3 years - but only if the company returns to profitability and has something to deduct.

Summary

Minimum CIT changes the way we think about taxes for companies. It is not enough to "have no profit" - you need to actively control profitability and understand when tax liability arises. Companies that analyze financial data throughout the year and plan ahead have the tools to act. Companies that ignore the issue until they submit CIT-8 - pay tax that could have been avoided.
