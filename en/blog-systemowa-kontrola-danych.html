Systemic data control – consistency of records in 2026

KSeF, JPK VAT and JPK CIT increase the importance of data consistency. Check what this means for records, documents and work organization in the company.

Accounting March 28, 2026 Liber Pro Accounting

KSeF, JPK CIT, JPK VAT and other data sources no longer operate in practice as separate areas. From the company's point of view, the importance of consistency of records, completeness of documents and the quality of data transferred between systems is increasing. It is the order in the data, and not a one-off reaction to control, that is becoming the most important element of accounting security today.

What is the change in approach to data?

So far

1

2

3

4

Mass analysis – errors detected algorithmically, without on-site inspection

The three layers of the system – how they work together in practice

layer 1

KSeF

Real-time data – every invoice

Each B2B invoice enters the KSeF system before it reaches the buyer. The tax office sees it immediately - NIP of the seller and buyer, amount, VAT, date. If someone does not collect an invoice from KSeF, or if a different amount appears in JPK VAT than in KSeF, the system detects it automatically.

layer 2

JPK VAT

Monthly verification – seller vs buyer

Each JPK VAT file is compared with the contractor's JPK VAT file. If the seller shows an invoice for PLN 10,000 VAT and the buyer does not show it at all or shows a different amount - the discrepancy is automatically flagged. Scale of analysis: millions of invoices per month, without human intervention.

layer 3

JPK CIT

Annual analysis – the entire structure of the books

Once a year, the office receives a complete picture of the accounting books - every account, every entry, every connection with a document. The system compares balance sheet results with the CIT declaration, looks for inconsistencies in depreciation, debt financing costs and transfers to related entities.

Error repeatability and data risk profile

The analytical systems of the Ministry of Finance do not treat all taxpayers equally. Based on data from JPK VAT, KSeF and JPK CIT, the so-called risk profile – a number that determines the probability of inspection. The higher the risk profile, the higher the company is placed on the list of entities to be verified.

NISKI

Data consistent between systems, no corrections, regular settlements, constant profitability. The company is virtually invisible to analytical systems.

MEDIUM

Occasional adjustments, minor discrepancies, low profitability in one year. Possible request for explanation - but without automatic control.

WYSOKI

Reproducible errors, large discrepancies between systems, historical changes in data, anomalies in the cost structure. Priority for verification or inspection.

Why is it an organizational change and not just a technical one?

Over the last 30 years of the Polish tax system, entrepreneurs have had some space. The office knew as much as it was told in the declaration. Control required decisions, resources and time. Errors could lie for years without being detected.

This environment is becoming a thing of the past. Not because the recipe has changed - but because it has information model. The office is no longer waiting for the declaration. He sees data before the company declares it. He compares them with contractors' data. Analyzes patterns over time. And he does it on a scale that no tax inspector could ever achieve.

Tax audits are no longer an event.
It became a process.

In the past: the office initiated an inspection → it was revealed whether the company had any errors.
Today: the system detects errors → the office initiates an inspection because it already knows what it will find.

What does this mean in practice for the company?

Data integrity becomes an operational responsibility

It is not enough that something is "booked". It must be recorded consistently with KSeF, JPK VAT and the contractor's data. Any discrepancy – even technical and innocuous – generates an alarm signal. An accounting office must act as a data consistency controller, not only as a "tax counter".

Manual processes become a risk

Manually entering invoices, manual date corrections, manual reconciliation of balances - each of these operations is a potential source of discrepancies visible to the system. Automation is no longer a convenience – it is risk management.

Profitability must be monitored throughout the year

Minimum CIT, profitability analysis in JPK CIT, risk profiles - all this means that the company's financial result cannot be a surprise only revealed at the end of the year. Quarterly or monthly monitoring is becoming a standard for tax risk management.

Corrections require thought and documentation

Every JPK correction and every historical change in the books is now visible to the analytical system. Corrections are still possible and permissible - but they must be informed, documented and consistent with all associated data structures.

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Does the tax office see my data on an ongoing basis?

Yes. Through KSeF (each invoice in real time), JPK VAT (monthly) and JPK CIT (once a year with a full picture of the books), the office has access to the company's financial data without the need for on-site inspections.

Does every inconsistency constitute control?

No. Isolated incidental discrepancies rarely result in an audit. The problem occurs when they create a repeating pattern or are large in amount. The systems classify taxpayers according to their level of risk.

What should I do to ensure that the company is prepared?

Three steps: (1) data consistency between KSeF, JPK VAT and books; (2) automatic receipt of invoices from KSeF instead of manual entry; (3) profitability monitoring throughout the year.

Can an accounting office help?

Yes, and this is the key role of the accounting office in the new environment. Liber Pro monitors the integrity of customer data and informs about risks before they become a problem.

Summary

KSeF, JPK CIT and JPK VAT are not three separate obligations to be met. These are three layers of one system that changed the tax control model in Poland. Companies that understand this – and organize their processes accordingly – will operate calmly. Companies that treat each change separately and react only when a problem occurs will increasingly be on the defensive.

The question is no longer "will the office inspect me?" It reads: "what does the analytical system see in my data and does what it sees raise any doubts?"
