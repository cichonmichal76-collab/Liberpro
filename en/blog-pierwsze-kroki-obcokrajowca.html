First Steps for a Foreigner in the Polish Tax System

A practical guide to legal work, PESEL, contracts, PIT, ZUS and first tax matters in Poland.

Taxes 15 August 2026 Liber Pro Accounting

Have you arrived in Poland and want to work or start a business here? Start by organising a few basic formalities — you do not need to understand every regulation at once.

1. Check whether you can work legally

The right to stay in Poland and the right to work are not always the same. Depending on your citizenship and residence documents, you may need a visa, a residence card, a work permit or a declaration from your employer.

If you are an EU citizen, the rules are generally simpler. If you come from outside the EU, check your documents before starting work.

Do my documents allow me to work legally in Poland?

2. Obtain a PESEL number

PESEL is an identification number used when dealing with Polish offices, an employer, a doctor and a bank. You may receive it when registering your address or submit a separate application at the city or municipal office if you cannot register your address.

Prepare an identity document, proof of legal stay and—if requested by the office—a document confirming your address.

3. Understand how you will work

Employment contract

You are an employee of a company. The employer calculates and pays the required tax and contributions, and you receive the amount left after the deductions.

Contract of mandate

You perform specific tasks for a company. Tax and insurance rules depend, among other things, on your personal situation and other insurance titles.

Your own business

You issue invoices and are responsible for your own taxes, contributions, deadlines and records. Do not start a business only because a client or employer says it will be “easier”. Compare the costs and obligations first.

4. Learn four basic terms

PIT

PIT is personal income tax. With an employment or mandate contract, the payer usually handles advance tax payments, but after the year ends you may still need to review and approve your annual return.

ZUS

ZUS administers social insurance. Under a contract, the payer usually handles the contributions. With your own business, you must manage registrations, declarations and deadlines yourself.

Health insurance

Ask your employer whether you have been registered for health insurance. With your own business, the health contribution is a separate obligation and should not be confused with social insurance contributions.

VAT

VAT is mainly relevant when you run a business, issue invoices or sell goods and services. It is not a basic tax issue for an employee working under a contract.

5. Check your first payslip

Gross pay is the amount before deductions. Net pay is the amount actually transferred to your bank account. If your contract states PLN 6,000 gross, it does not automatically mean that PLN 6,000 will reach your account.

Ask for a document showing gross pay, tax, contributions and net pay. This is often called a payslip.

6. Keep your documents

  • your employment or mandate contract,
  • payment confirmations and payslips,
  • residence documents and confirmation of your PESEL number,
  • tax and social insurance information,
  • the PIT-11 information received from your employer,
  • documents concerning income earned outside Poland.

Keep both paper copies and secure scans or photographs.

7. Check your PIT-11 after the year ends

PIT-11 shows your income, tax withheld and contributions. It is used to prepare your annual tax return. A PIT return is generally filed between 15 February and 30 April of the following year—through Twój e-PIT or with help from an accountant.

Current information is published by the Ministry of Finance.

8. Tell your accountant about income from other countries

Tell your accountant about salary from a foreign company, remote work, a foreign pension, rental income, dividends, interest, investments or a business operated outside Poland.

This does not automatically mean that you will pay tax twice. Poland has tax treaties with many countries, but you still need to establish where and how the income should be reported.

What can you do today?

  1. Check whether you can work legally.
  2. Obtain a PESEL number.
  3. Read your contract before signing it.
  4. Confirm whether the stated pay is gross or net.
  5. Check your health insurance registration.
  6. Keep all documents.
  7. Tell your accountant about income earned outside Poland.
  8. Do not start a business before checking the taxes and contributions involved.
