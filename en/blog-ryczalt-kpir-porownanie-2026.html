Lump sum or KPiR – comparison of settlement rules in 2026

Comparison of the lump sum and KPiR: costs, health insurance premium, method of recording and situations that most often influence the choice of the settlement form.

Taxes May 25, 2021 Liber Pro Accounting

The choice between a lump sum and the Revenue and Expenditure Book (KPiR) affects the method of recording revenues and expenses, the amount of charges and the organization of documents in the company. From the perspective of an accounting office, the most important thing is to compare the real cost structure, the way of doing business and the total charges, and not to make a choice based on simplified calculators or general opinions.

What is the difference between a lump sum and KPiR?

Lump sumKPiR (scale / linear)
Tax baseRevenueIncome (income – costs)
Obtaining costsNoneYes – full deduction
Rates2-17% depending on the industry12%/32% (scale) or 19% (linear)
Health insurance premiumFrom income thresholdsFrom income
SimplicityHighAverage
OptimizationLimitedGreat possibilities

Key Difference: Do you have costs and how big are they? A lump sum ignores costs - you pay for every PLN of income. KPiR rewards costs - the more justified expenses, the lower the tax.

Comparative example – when costs are more important

Let's take a specific case:

  • Revenue: PLN 20,000 per month
  • Costs: PLN 8,000 per month (equipment, fuel, leasing, office)
FormCalculationTax/monthTax/year
Lump sum 12%20 000 × 12%PLN 2,400PLN 28,800
KPiR linear 19%(20 000. 8 000) × 19%PLN 2,280PLN 27,360
DifferencePLN 120PLN 1,440

Already with a 40% share of costs, KPiR begins to be competitive. But that's not the whole picture. In addition, there is a health insurance contribution (varies depending on the form), the possibility of depreciation of fixed assets and other optimization elements. In practice, the differences may be several thousand PLNs per year in favor of one form or the other.

Comparative example – when low costs are important

Another scenario. Freelancer without high costs:

  • Revenue: PLN 10,000 per month
  • Costs: PLN 500 per month (telephone and internet only)
FormCalculationTax/monthTax/year
Lump sum 12%10 000 × 12%PLN 1,200PLN 14,400
KPiR linear 19%(10 000. 500) × 19%PLN 1,805PLN 21,660
DifferencePLN 605PLN 7,260

When costs are low, a lump sum is definitely better. A programmer, graphic designer or consultant without large fixed costs can save on a lump sum over PLN 7,000 per year compared to a flat tax.

When is a lump sum payment most often chosen?

A lump sum is useful when:

  • Here you go very low costs activities (less than 20-30% of revenues)
  • You operate in the service industry. IT, marketing, consulting, copywriting
  • You don't regularly invest in equipment or leasing
  • You care about simplicity of accounting and less documentation
  • Your flat rate is low (e.g. 8.5% for many services, 5.5% for construction works)

Typical example: freelancer without costs, programmer working remotely, consultant without an office or employees.

When do entrepreneurs choose KPiR more often?

KPiR starts winning when:

  • Here you go high costs. leasing, equipment, fuel, materials
  • You invest regularly in the development of the company
  • You employ employees (salaries are a cost)
  • You want optimize tax and have a complete picture of your finances
  • You are planning to apply for a loan - banks assess KPiR better

Typical example: a construction company, a shop, a manufacturing company, a doctor's office with the costs of maintaining the premises and staff.

Health insurance premium – an element of comparison that should not be omitted

In 2026, the health insurance premium is still an important element of the calculation:

  • On a lump sum basis the contribution is calculated based on the income thresholds: PLN 376.16 (up to PLN 60,000 of income), PLN 627.08 (PLN 60,001 - 300,000), PLN 1,128.48 (above PLN 300,000) - indicative amounts for 2026.
  • On KPiR (linear) the contribution is 4.9% of income, not less than the minimum
  • On KPiR (scale) the contribution is 9% of income

In many cases, it is the health insurance premium that determines which form is more advantageous. At high incomes, a flat tax (4.9% of health insurance contributions) may be better than a scale (9%) - despite a higher tax rate of 19% vs. 12%.

The most common mistakes of entrepreneurs when choosing a form

Mistake 1. Choosing "because someone said so"

Each business has a different cost structure. What works for a fellow programmer may not work for you as a consultant with a car lease and a rented office.

Mistake 2. Not updating the form every year

The company changes, but the form of taxation remains the same. If you started investing in equipment or hired employees, your optimal form may have changed.

Mistake 3. Not taking into account the health insurance premium

Comparing only the tax rate without health insurance premiums is a mistake that can cost several thousand PLNs a year.

Mistake 4. Decision without annual simulation

The form of taxation is valid all year round. A decision made "on a monthly basis" or without a 12-month projection carries the risk of overpaying tax throughout the year.

Can I change the form of taxation?

Yes - but only on a specific date. Changes in form must be reported by the 20th day of the month following the month in which you achieved your first income in a given tax year.

After this date, the decision is valid for the entire tax year and cannot be changed during the year. That is why it is so important to undertake it consciously - preferably based on a tax simulation prepared by an accounting office.

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Is a lump sum always cheaper?

No. Only at low cost. When costs are high, KPiR with a flat or scaled tax may result in a lower tax.

Is KPiR more complicated?

Yes, it requires documentation of costs. However, it provides greater optimization opportunities and better transparency of the company's finances.

Is it possible to switch from a lump sum to KPiR?

Yes, from the new tax year. The change must be reported by the 20th of the month after receiving the first income in the new year.

Does the form of taxation affect creditworthiness?

Yes. Banks often evaluate KPiR better because income is more transparent. For lump sum payments, banks can use their own income conversion rates, which may be less favorable.

Summary

There is no single answer. The optimal choice depends on your cost structure, industry, investment plans and family situation. The best approach is an annual tax simulation that takes into account tax, health insurance premiums and all elements specific to your business.
