Changes in accounting entries after year-end closing – audit trail and JPK

Changing historical data in the books requires justification and an audit trail. Check how it affects documents and consistency with JPK.

JPK January 11, 2025 Liber Pro Accounting

Changes in accounting entries after the end of the year may be justified, but they require proper documentation, maintaining an audit trail and checking the impact on the records and JPK files. From the perspective of the accounting office, the most important are: the reason for the correction, the connection with the source document and the consistency of historical data after the change.

What does a change in accounting entries after the year-end close mean?

This concept is broader than it seems. Includes:

Changes to records

  • Changing the date of the source document
  • Changing the posting date
  • Account value adjustment
  • Change of account in the decree

Changes in documentation

  • Replacing a PDF document in the archive
  • Adding a document to an existing record
  • Change the pinning date of an attachment
  • Correction of the transaction description

Each of these operations may seem "technical" - in fact, each has potential tax consequences.

What do the regulations say about correcting records?

The Accounting Act requires that accounting books be reliable and error-free. Any change to records should be documented and traceable. So it's not about that czy can be changed - it's about jak i dlaczego.

Key principle

Changing a record is allowed if: an error is detected, there is substantive justification, the change is documented and an audit trail is maintained - that is, it is known who changed it, when and why. A change without documentation is a risk, not a correction.

How does changing one element affect the records?

Impact on VAT

Changing the date of the document may transfer the VAT deduction to another settlement period. If JPK_VAT for this period has already been submitted, an inconsistency arises requiring a correction of the declaration. An unsubmitted correction is an error in the VAT settlement.

Impact on CIT

Shifting the cost from December to January changes the financial result for the year, which has already been closed and settled for taxes. If the difference is significant, it is necessary to correct the CIT return for the previous year.

Impact on data consistency in JPK

A change in the accounting system without updating the related JPK structures creates a discrepancy visible to the office's analytical systems. This is a classic signal that can initiate control.

Practical example – changing the date of a document

Scenario:

The company has a cost invoice dated December 31. For various reasons, the date in the system is changed to January 2 of the following year.

What's changing

  • The cost carries over to the new year
  • The previous year's result is growing
  • VAT moves to the new period
  • JPK for December does not match the invoice

What the office sees in JPK CIT

  • Invoice date: December 31st
  • Date of entry in the book: January 2
  • Date in the VAT register: January 2
  • Date anomaly - alarm signal

Particular risk: technical changes without an audit trail

In many accounting and archiving systems it is technically possible to:

  • Changing the date on which a document is pinned to save
  • Replacing a PDF file without changing the save metadata
  • Modification of the transaction description after the period close

The problem is that such changes are not always visible at first glance - but they always leave a trace in the data. In the case of JPK CIT, where the office analyzes data structures automatically, date inconsistencies, replaced documents and anomalies in connections are detected algorithmically - without the need to physically inspect the documents.

The most common mistakes companies make when changing historical data

Error 1. "We only correct the date"

No analysis of the tax consequences of changing the date. The person performing the change sees the technical operation. The office sees potential manipulation of the settlement period.

Error 2. Lack of documentation of changes

A change without a record of who made it, when and why. During the inspection, the company is unable to explain the discrepancies between the data in the system and the source documents.

Error 3. Change after sending JPK without correction

The data in the system is changed, but the sent JPK_VAT is not corrected. There is a permanent discrepancy between what the office has in its database and what is in the company's system.

Error 4. Incompatibility of the archive with the system

A document in the accounting system is different from a document in a PDF archive. A replaced attachment without an audit trail is one of the most serious signals during data verification.

When is change allowed and how to make it?

A change is justified when an actual error is detected. The procedure should be as follows:

1

Document the change

Write down who makes the change, when and why. Preferably in writing - a memo or a record in the system with your user ID and date.

2

Analyze the tax impact

Does the change affect VAT? At CIT? For the previous year's result? Each of these areas may require correction of the declaration.

3

Update all related structures

A change in one place must be consistent with everything else: VAT register, document archive, fixed assets register, if applicable.

4

Submit a JPK correction if necessary

If the change concerns the period in which JPK_VAT has already been submitted, a correction must be submitted. Lack of correction with a changed database means a permanent discrepancy in the office's data.

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Can entries be changed after the end of the year?

Yes, but only in justified cases - when an error is detected and correction is necessary. Each change must be documented and an audit trail maintained.

Does changing the document date have tax implications?

Yes, important. It affects the VAT settlement period, the financial result for the year and the CIT basis. Even a change that is perceived as technical has real tax consequences.

Does the tax office see historical changes?

Yes, especially in JPK CIT. The office's analytical systems detect date inconsistencies, discrepancies between JPK structures and anomalies in document connections.

Is it possible to replace a PDF document in the system?

Technically yes, but it's a very big risk. If the document in the archive is different from the one that was the basis for the entry, the office may consider the entry as undocumented during the inspection.

Summary

Changes in the books are not technical operations. These are tax operations - each of them affects settlements, data consistency and may be visible to the tax office. In the JPK CIT era, this visibility is greater than ever before.

Companies that have clear procedures for corrections in their books, document every change and ensure data consistency have nothing to fear. Companies where changes are made ad hoc, without documentation and without analysis of the effects - expose themselves to serious risk during inspections.
