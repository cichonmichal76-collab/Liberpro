The most common errors in JPK VAT - what causes problems with the office

The office analyzes JPK VAT automatically. Errors are detected without on-site inspection. Check what most often generates calls and how to avoid it.

JPK February 11, 2022 Liber Pro Accounting

The JPK VAT file is today the basic tax control tool. The tax office analyzes it automatically - errors are detected without on-site inspection, without notice, without notice. The systems compare data en masse, detect patterns and generate alarm signals. Even minor irregularities repeated over several months may result in a request for clarification or the initiation of an inspection.

How does the tax office analyze JPK VAT?

The Ministry of Finance has analytical systems that compare JPK data between taxpayers, check consistency between the purchase register and the contractor's sales, detect repeatable error patterns and identify statistical anomalies. The key is that the analysis is there mass and automatic – does not require human presence. What previously required on-site inspection now takes seconds.

What the system analyzes

  • Compliance of purchase and sales invoices
  • Repeatability of errors in subsequent periods
  • Differences between declaration and register
  • Unusual values and anomalies

The result of the analysis

  • Call for explanations
  • Request to correct the declaration
  • Tax audit
  • Initiation of proceedings

The most common errors in JPK VAT

1. No cost invoice included

The most common

The invoice exists in KSeF or the archive, but it has not been recorded and has not been included in the VAT purchase register. After implementing KSeF, the office sees the invoice in the system - but at the same time does not see it in the taxpayer's JPK.

Effects: overstated VAT due to be paid · no cost in the tax bill · signal of discrepancy between KSeF and JPK

2. Double view of the invoice

The same invoice is posted twice - most often when entering data manually, when the invoice is received both from the system and from e-mail. As a result, the input VAT is overstated by the value of one invoice.

Effects: overstated input VAT · need to correct the declaration · difference detected by comparison with the contractor's data

3. Incorrect dates – issuance and posting

Wrong invoice issue date (e.g. December instead of January) or incorrect date of inclusion in the VAT register. Even a difference of one day at the turn of the month shifts VAT to a different settlement period.

Effects: VAT transfer between periods · inconsistency between subsequent JPKs · anomaly visible in historical analysis

4. Inconsistency with data in KSeF

After implementing the mandatory KSeF, the office compares invoices in the system with the data submitted in JPK. An invoice modified after issuance, a changed date or a different NIP - any discrepancy is detected automatically.

Effects: signal for risk analysis · request to clarify discrepancies · risk of questioning VAT deduction

5. Incorrect GTU designations and procedures

Incorrect GTU codes (groups of goods and services), lack of mandatory VAT procedure markings (TP, MK, MPP, SW) or incorrect classification of transactions. This is one of the most common reasons for calls for explanations.

Effects: request for explanations · necessity to correct JPK · possible fines for lack of markings

6. Data corrections without JPK correction

The data in the accounting system is changed after sending the JPK, but the declaration correction is not submitted. The office has old data from JPK - the company is working on new ones. The discrepancy increases with each passing month.

Effects: permanent data inconsistency · audit risk · necessity of retroactive corrections for several periods at the same time

A practical example - an invoice at the turn of the period

Scenario:

The company receives a cost invoice in December. The administration department submits it to accounting with a delay - it is recorded in January of the following year.

Tax effect

  • Input VAT postponed until January
  • December – inflated amount to be paid
  • The difference between the fiscal and fiscal years

What the office sees

  • Invoice in KSeF – December
  • Inclusion in JPK VAT – January
  • Date discrepancy → alarm signal

How to avoid errors in JPK VAT?

1

Automation – integration with KSeF via API

Automatic invoice downloading from KSeF eliminates manual entries, double entries and incorrect dates. The accounting program loads the data itself - without human intervention.

2

Regular reconciliations before period close

Before sending JPK - verification: is each cost invoice from KSeF included in the purchase register? Does the VAT due match the sales invoices? Reconciliation takes a moment and eliminates most errors.

3

Control of GTU markings and VAT procedures

Verification whether each transaction has the correct markings. Particularly important in the case of new contractors, new products/services and transactions with related entities (TP).

4

Internal procedures – who is responsible for what

Clear rules: who is responsible for timely delivery of invoices to the accounting office, who verifies the data before sending the JPK, who decides on the correction. Without procedures, responsibility becomes blurred.

Is JPK correction a problem?

No - if it is done consciously, quickly and consistently. The office understands that mistakes happen. The problem begins when corrections are frequent, concern many periods at the same time or indicate a lack of control over the data. This builds the taxpayer's "risk profile" in the analytical system.

Correction is fine when

  • It is incidental and quick
  • Covers reasonable error
  • It is consistent with other data
  • Does not create further incompatibilities

Correction is a risk when

  • It is repeated every month
  • It covers many previous periods
  • Creates a pattern of risk
  • It is not explained in the documentation

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Does every error in JPK VAT result in an audit?

No. A single incidental error rarely results in an audit. The problem occurs when errors are repeated and form a pattern. It is the repetition that is the main risk factor in the mass analysis of the office.

Does the office see invoices from KSeF and compare them with JPK?

Yes. After implementing the mandatory KSeF, the office has access to all invoices issued and received by the taxpayer. It automatically compares them with the data in the JPK - discrepancies are detected algorithmically.

Can I correct JPK after sending it?

Yes, by correcting JPK. Correction itself is not a problem - as long as it is done consciously, quickly and consistently. The problem occurs when corrections are frequent and uncontrolled.

Does manual posting increase the risk of errors?

Yes, significantly. Manual data entry is the most common source of errors in JPK VAT - double entries, incorrect dates, mistakes in the Tax Identification Number. Automatic integration with KSeF via API eliminates most of these errors.

Summary

JPK VAT is not just a file to be sent once a month. This is the main source of analytical data for the tax office - available in real time, analyzed automatically, compared with KSeF and other JPK structures. Companies that have structured processes, automation and regular reconciliations operate without problems. Companies with chaos in document flow generate corrections, notices and - in the case of repeatable errors - the risk of audits.
