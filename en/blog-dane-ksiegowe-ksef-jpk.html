How accounting data work in a company - KSeF, JPK and books in practice

Today, KSeF, JPK VAT, JPK CIT and accounting books form one data system. See how it works in practice and where discrepancies most often arise.

Data control April 19, 2026 Liber Pro Accounting

The company's accounting data no longer exists in one place. Invoices go through KSeF, entries are entered into the VAT records and accounting books, and then they are reported in the JPK structures. In practice, most problems do not result from a single document, but from inconsistencies between these data layers.

What does an accounting data system consist of?

At least three areas of data coexist in the company's daily work: source documents, accounting records and reporting. KSeF acts as a source of structured invoices, VAT records and accounting books organize economic events, and JPK transfers data to the reporting format.

KSeF

Source of sales invoices and receipt of cost invoices in a structured model.

Records and books

VAT registers, KPiR or accounting books in which economic events are recorded.

JPK

The reporting layer, which requires consistency with records and source documentation.

How data flows in practice

The most common pattern looks similar in most companies. The document is created or sent to the organization, then it is assigned to the records, recorded in the appropriate period and reported. The more manual steps, the greater the risk of differences between systems.

StageWhat's going onWhere does the problem most often arise?
1. DocumentThe invoice is issued or receivedNo download, duplicates, data discrepancy
2. RecordsThe document is entered into the VAT register or booksIncorrect date, missing document, incorrect markings
3. ReportingThe data is reported in JPKIncomplete compliance with records or historical changes

Where data inconsistencies most often occur

Repeatable discrepancies usually do not result from a single recipe, but from the process. The most common sources include lack of complete receipt of invoices, manual data rewriting, changes made after the period close, and lack of control between the source of the document and the records.

  • the invoice exists in KSeF, but has not been included in the records
  • the same document was included more than once
  • Document dates and capture dates vary between systems
  • the relevant reports were not updated after the books were corrected
  • The PDF or other visualization does not match the source data

KSeF as an element of a larger data system

KSeF does not operate independently of accounting. In practice, it affects the method of receiving documents, the completeness of records, the timeliness of data transfer and their compliance with subsequent reporting. Therefore, after implementing KSeF, the most important thing is to organize the document flow, and not just to issue an invoice.

JPK and books – why consistency matters

Reporting does not create data from scratch. JPK reflects what is included in the records. If historical changes are made to the books and the process does not leave an audit trail, differences between documents, records and reports increase. From the organization's perspective, the most important thing is the consistency of data over time.

Related articles: errors in JPK VAT, changes in accounting entries after year-end closing, JPK CIT – preparation of books and data.

Basic data integrity checklist

1. Source documents

Check whether all documents from KSeF and other sources have been entered into the correct circulation and have been transferred to accounting.

2. Records

Verify the completeness of the recognition, date, markings and the absence of duplicates in the VAT records or books.

3. Historical changes

Ensure that each correction leaves an audit trail and is consistent with the source documentation.

4. Reporting

Compare the data in the JPK with the registers, books and the period they concern.

Summary

Today's accounting is not a single program, but a set of interconnected data sources. KSeF, JPK VAT, JPK CIT and accounting books describe the same events from different perspectives. The better the document flow and consistency control are, the easier it is to maintain the completeness of records and reduce subsequent corrections.
