JPK CIT – how to prepare books and data in the company

JPK CIT increases the importance of the quality of accounting data. Check how to prepare books, chart of accounts, documents and audit trail.

JPK August 26, 2024 Liber Pro Accounting

JPK CIT means an increase in the importance of data quality in accounting books. From the company's perspective, the most important things become: consistency of records, an orderly chart of accounts, proper connection with source documents and maintaining an audit trail during corrections. It is worth starting preparation for new responsibilities by reviewing the current organization of accounting data.

What is JPK CIT?

JPK_KR_PD

Accounting book with tax data. It contains all accounting entries in a standardized digital structure - journal, synthetic and analytical accounts, balance in each account.

JPK_ST

Records of fixed assets and intangible assets. It contains data on each fixed asset, value, depreciation and the method of including write-offs as costs.

Collectively, the tax office will receive a detailed picture of the entire accounting of the company - not only the resulting numbers in the return, but the structure that produced them.

What is actually changing on the data side?

So farAfter the implementation of JPK CIT
What the office seesTax returnsAccounting entries, account structure, documents
The nature of controlSpot, on demandContinuous data analysis

Inconsistency toleranceBigMinimal

What does this mean in practice for the company?

1. The end of "loose" accounting

There is no longer room for inconsistencies between documents and records, manual corrections without justification, or "equalizing" balances at the end of the period without documentation. Each operation must be consistent with the source document and logically justified.

2. Automatic data analysis by office systems

The tax office will not read the books manually. Analytical systems will automatically detect repeatable errors, unusual operations and discrepancies between various JPK structures. Irregularities will be detected faster than ever before.

3. Data quality is crucial

It is not enough that something has been recorded. What matters is how it was posted - on the right account, on the right date, with the right connection to the document. JPK CIT verifies not only the values, but the entire logic of the entry.

The greatest operational risks in JPK CIT

Risk 1. Data inconsistency between systems

Different data in the accounting book, different in the VAT register, different in the declaration. Any discrepancy will be visible to the office's analytical system and may result in a request for clarification or an inspection.

Risk 2. Historical changes in records

Corrections to document dates, changes to values entered retroactively, modifications after the period is closed - this is especially visible for analytical systems. The practice of "correcting" historical records without justification is one of the main alarm signals.

Risk 3. Lack of quality control procedures

The company does not have specific rules on who can make corrections, who verifies records and how changes are documented. Without these procedures, it is difficult to prove that the changes were justified.

A practical example of data discrepancies

Scenario:

The company issues an invoice with the date of March 31, but for various reasons changes the date in the system to April 1 (a different VAT settlement period). Does not update all related records.

Effect in JPK CIT:

  • Invoice date in one structure: March 31
  • Date in the accounting entry: April 1
  • Date in the VAT register: April 1

The office's analytical system detects date discrepancies in related documents. A classic alarm signal - a potential pretext to initiate an inspection.

How to prepare a company for JPK CIT?

1Organization and consistency of accounting

  • Review and reconciliation of all account balances
  • Verification of compliance of entries with source documents
  • Reconciliation of data between VAT registers and declarations

2Control of accounting processes

  • Defining who is authorized to make entries
  • Defining the procedure for verification and authorization of corrections
  • Implementation of the principle of documenting every historical change

3Limiting historical changes

  • Historical corrections only in justified, documented cases
  • Blocking the possibility of modifying closed periods without authorization
  • Audit trail for every change to historical data

4Regular arrangements

  • Monthly reconciliation of the VAT register with the JPK_VAT declaration
  • Regular reconciliation of settlement balances (receivables, payables)
  • Quarterly verification of fixed assets records

5Verification of the accounting system

  • Checking whether the system supports export in the JPK CIT format
  • Verification of correct configuration of charts of accounts
  • Data consistency test before the first mandatory report

Does every company need to prepare?

Yes. Although the obligation is being implemented in stages and starts with larger entities, it will eventually cover most companies that keep full accounting books. Implementation schedule:

  • Large entities (revenue over EUR 50 million) - first stages of implementation
  • Medium-sized entities — next stages
  • Other companies with full accounting — eventually everyone

Companies that start preparations earlier have time to calmly organize the data. Companies that wait until the last minute will have to operate under time pressure - with the risk of errors and non-compliance.

The most common mistakes made by companies

  • Treating JPK CIT as "another file to send" — without understanding that it requires a change in the quality of all accounting
  • No data quality control — "uploading" documents without verifying the correctness of accounts and dates
  • Ignoring historical changes — failure to understand that any retroactive modification is visible to the system
  • No internal procedures — lack of clear rules on who can change what and how to document it

FAQ – most frequently asked questions

Does JPK CIT mean higher taxes?

Not directly. JPK CIT does not change the rates or tax base. However, it increases the detection of errors and inconsistencies, which may lead to corrections and additional tax assessment.

Will the office see all postings?

Yes, to a large extent. JPK CIT includes entries in the accounting book, account structure and connections between documents. The office receives a detailed picture of all accounting.

Can historical data be corrected in the books?

Yes, but only with justification and full documentation of the changes. Historical changes are particularly visible for analytical systems. Corrections should only be made in justified cases, with full documentation.

Does JPK CIT apply only to large companies?

NO. The obligation is being implemented in stages, but will eventually cover most companies that keep full accounting books. Preparation should start earlier than the obligation for a given group.

Summary

JPK CIT is a fundamental change in the approach to tax audit - from auditing declarations to analyzing the entire accounting. Companies that have organized data, consistent processes and high quality records will go through the implementation without problems. Companies with chaos in their books will have serious difficulties.

Preparation doesn't have to be revolutionary. It starts with an audit of the current state of the books, identifying risk areas and systematically organizing data. The earlier, the calmer.
